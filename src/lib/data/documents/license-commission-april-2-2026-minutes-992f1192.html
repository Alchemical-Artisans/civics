3. CONTINUED BUSINESSS

3.1 Evan Phillips & Brad Patnaude

Adamson Industries, Corp.

d/b/a Adamson Industries, Corp.

45 Research Drive

-Item for Discussion: Status of Motor Vehicle Dealer License

-Continued from March 5, 2026 meeting

Brad Patnaude, Treasurer and acting Manager, and Atty. Bryan E. Chase, representing Adamson Industries, were present for this discussion. Atty. Alexander Castro, City Solicitor associate, appeared remotely on behalf of the Commission. Atty. Chase reviewed questions raised at the previous meeting and said he understood Adamson had submitted invoices since then. Atty. Chase stated that the sale of vehicles only constituted 7.5% of the business’ revenue, that only 25 cars were sold in the previous 12 months and 21 of these were new. Their main business is the upfitting of vehicles for emergency personnel. But the attorney added that since a dealer license was required to sell vehicles and most of their sales were new vehicles, the Class I license Adamson currently holds is most appropriate. Atty. Castro advised that Class I licenses were intended for dealerships such as AutoFair and it was difficult to “find a box” to put this business in since the statute “is clear” about dealer licenses applying to businesses “whose principal business” is the sale of vehicles. He stated that the decision was ultimately the License Commission’s to make but that he couldn’t see how

Adamson would qualify for a Class I or II license, perhaps a Class III as their business involved remodeling vehicles before selling them. Chairperson Angus noted that another factor for a Class I license was to be an authorized agent of a manufacturer, which was also absent here. She inquired how the business typically acquires its new vehicles. Mr. Patnaude stated it was “typically dealer-to-dealer transfers.” Atty. Castro asked the party if they believed this constituted as being a “recognized agent”. Atty. Chase stated he believed it did fall under that range. He added that he had also reviewed the statute and did not think Class II or III applied since Adamson did not buy second- hand vehicles. Atty. Castro stated he mainly referred to the “remodeling” factor when he proposed Class III. Atty. Chase replied he did not think any class was a precise fit for Adamson but that a dealer license would be necessary for it to sell cars, and the “vast majority” sold were new. Atty. Castro noted that the invoices submitted demonstrated that selling new vehicles was not the principal business. Atty. Chase agreed but maintained that of the few vehicles sold in the past year, most were new. Atty. Castro granted that there was an argument for a Class I with respect to that detail, and restated that the ultimate decision was the License Commission’s. Commissioner Koutoulas stated she believed this situation qualified the business for a Class I, in the absence of a different license that truly fit their business needs. Chairperson Angus noted there was no contract between Adamson and a manufacturer and asked Mr. Patnaude if they always buy from the same brand. Mr. Patnaude said it was a variety. The Chairperson wondered how the license could be completed if they stayed with Class I. Atty. Castro noted there were multiple makes in the invoices. Atty. Chase stated that all 21 new vehicles sold in the past year were Ford vehicles. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if Adamson had any direct interaction with the manufacturers. Mr. Patnaude replied they were an approved “dropship” location and occasionally receive vehicles directly from the manufacturer’s factory, whereupon they will do “dealer prep”. Commissioner Koutoulas said she believed this constituted as having an agreement with the manufacturer – Atty. Chase agreed this supported the “recognized agent” aspect. Chairperson Angus inquired if there was one or more manufacturers. Atty. Chase replied that for all new vehicles sold in the past year, it was only Ford. Mr. Patnaude stated that this could theoretically occur with Dodge or GM as well, being an approved dropship location. He added that a business technically had to be a recognized dealer to be a dropship location. Commissioner Driscoll mentioned his concern that granting a class other than I could restrict their business and said he was fine with granting Class I. Atty. Castro restated the concern from the City Solicitor’s office was that selling new vehicles was not the principal business, but left it to the Commission’s discretion.

Motion: Commissioner Koutoulas motions to approve the reissuance of a Class I license for the current Adamson Industries, Corp, in the absence of clarity in the M.G.L. Chapter 140 statute.

Second: Commissioner Driscoll seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.

-Application for new Motor Vehicle Dealer Class II license

-Has Building Inspector and Fire Department approval

-Pending Police Department approval

Andy Raposo, applicant, was present for this application. Chairperson Angus remarked that the previously submitted plot plan, reused from the location’s previous business, needed to be replaced with the new plan the applicant just submitted shortly before the meeting. She explained that the Commission decided several years back

that all new applicants needed to acquire their own plot plan, due to potential code changes, issues with former owners, and giving a chance to zoning departments to verify compliance. The Chairperson noted that the new plan had a couple of discrepancies compared to the previous plan and asked that the applicant’s engineer revise the plan to be in compliance with the allotted vehicle numbers: 80 for sale/service, two for employees, three for customers. She also cautioned the applicant that the City has an active Traffic & Safety Department and will routinely inspect dealerships for compliance. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if Mr. Raposo was aware of Lemon Law stickers and he said he was. Chairperson Angus asked about the applicant’s experience – Mr. Raposo replied he had sold vehicles for eight years and previously worked in Plaistow, NH and Methuen. The Chairperson asked if the applicant was well-versed in the logbook requirements and Mr. Raposo confirmed so. Commissioner Koutoulas noted that plot plans typically also include a snow storage area and asked that the engineer denote this area in the revision. Mr. Raposo mentioned that snow was currently stored next to the garage. Chairperson Angus denoted the area that must be left clear for emergency vehicle access. Mr. Raposo said he would work on revising the plan accordingly with his engineer. Discussion followed on whether to issue a temporary license or table the application until the revised plot plan is submitted. Lt. Lynch said he was fine with a temporary license. Chairperson Angus cautioned the applicant that the former business owner would illegally park his vehicles on the street and to take care to stay within his footprint – Mr. Raposo agreed. 13. SHOW CAUSE HEARING

13.1 Jonus Rustani, Manager

The New Lantern Cafe, Inc.

d/b/a New Lantern Cafe

240 Essex Street

-Show Cause Hearing:

• Alleged violation of 204 CMR 2.05 (2) Permitting an illegality on the

licensed premises to wit

• Alleged violation of M.G.L. Ch.138 §2 – Unlawful sale for off-premises

consumption

• Alleged violation of M.G.L. Ch.138 §12 – Sale of alcoholic beverages to be

drunk on the licensed premises

-Possible overserving

-Incident that occurred on or about February 28, 2026, at approximately 8:23PM

Lt. Kevin Lynch presented the facts. Officers were dispatched for a reported disturbance. Witnesses advised a female patron brought a drink outside and was told by the victim she could not do so; a fight ensued, the female patron threw the drink at the victim and punched her several times. The victim was brought to the hospital to have her injuries treated. The owner advised the police that he did not witness the fight but that there was a slight altercation inside the bar. Witnesses advised the patron had 3-5 drinks, and the victim had several drinks. The lieutenant acquired video, could not witness the fight on the video but did witness the drink being brought outside. He also noticed other violations such as people vaping, which he reported to the Board of Health, and employees smoking inside after-hours.

Jonus Rustani was sworn in. Commissioner Koutoulas asked Lt. Lynch if the business had working cameras in the back alley – the lieutenant said there were two but they did not pick up the incident. He also confirmed there were cameras inside the business but the time is off by one hour. Chairperson Angus inquired how many bartenders were on duty – Mr. Rustani stated there were two. The Chairperson asked why patrons were allowed to take drinks outdoors – the manager replied he did not witness this but heard about the victim telling her aggressor she could not bring a drink outside. The Chairperson asked if the manager watched exiting patrons to ensure they did not remove alcohol from the premises. The manager replied he does as much as he can – Commissioner Koutoulas remarked this is why the Commission mandated having two bartenders 4 days a week. Chairperson Angus asked if there were a lot of people

in the bar that night and Mr. Rustani stated there were “quite a few.” The Chairperson asked how long the woman had been in the bar – Lt. Lynch advised she’d been there several hours. The lieutenant also stated that he did see footage of Mr. Rustani asking the woman to leave but that she also vaped right in front of him. Mr. Rustani said he could “put a stop to all the vaping” – Lt. Lynch and Chairperson Angus advised it was a violation and he should not have to be told to enforce it.

Commissioner Koutoulas stated she had many issues, citing the different alleged violations, history of Show Cause hearings for New Lantern Cafe, and the business’ current probation. She declared she found this “extremely disturbing because […] nothing changes.” Chairperson Angus asked if the fight outside the bar happened after the altercation inside – Mr. Rustani stated he believed the outside fight happened first. The manager further stated that when he saw the aggressor inside make another attempt to go after the victim, he forced her and her boyfriend outside the bar – the defendant allegedly threw ice and snow at him in response. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if he called the police at any point – Mr. Rustani said he was busy “getting them out” but the police did show up, though he did not know who called the police. Chairperson Angus inquired if the back area was accessible to the public or only patrons – the manager advised the back of the building was accessible via High St. Commissioner Koutoulas asked why neither bartender noticed drinks being taken outside. Mr. Rustani said he had been “working sick” since February 14th, that his health wasn’t at 100% and he was doing the best he could. The Commissioner declared she wished to close the business for two weeks. Mr. Rustani protested that he would have no income and wouldn’t be able to pay bills. The Commission noted that these violations kept occurring despite repeated sanctions; Commissioner Koutoulas emphasized that a person was injured. The manager requested one week of closure but Commissioner Koutoulas did not sway, citing the Commission’s responsibility to protect the public. Mr. Rustani asked how he was supposed to protect himself against an incident that suddenly occurs with no forewarning – Commissioner Driscoll countered that New Lantern Cafe was the only licensee out of 60 Section 12 licensees to be summoned every other month. Mr. Rustani advised he wished to sell the business – the Commissioner urged him to do so and stated he believed the license should be revoked. Commissioner Koutoulas verified if he wished to make that motion but Commissioner Driscoll declined. Commissioner Koutoulas noted that Mr. Rustani was cited as having witnessed the indoor altercation and asked if he ever called the police himself. Mr. Rustani replied he expulsed the aggressor’s party outside the business – the Commissioner underlined that the manager allowed the drunk individuals to drive away instead of calling the police.

The manager replied that the police arrived at the time he expulsed them but the Commissioner noted this was luck and not due to his actions.

Motion: Commissioner Koutoulas motions to close New Lantern Cafe for business for two weeks, from April 3 through April 17, 2026.

Second: Commissioner Driscoll seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.

Chairperson Angus instructed Mr. Rustani to turn in his liquor license to the City Clerk’s office the next day and advised he could collect it again on April 17th End of Day, not to reopen before April 18th.

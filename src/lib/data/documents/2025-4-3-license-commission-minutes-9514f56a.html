13.2 Bassam Geha, Manager

Michael's Function Hall, Inc.

d/b/a Michael's Function Hall & The Phoenician Restaurant

12 Alpha Street

-Item for Discussion: Alleged disturbance on or about March 21, 2025

Sergeant Kevin Lynch presented the facts. On March 21st, 2025, at approximately 11PM, officers were dispatched to the business for a reported disturbance during a band performance by an employee named Michael Murphy. The officers met with the employee and parties involved outside, one male and one female. The calling party stated they had two drinks each before they became unruly. They chose to not press charges as the only damage was broken glasses. The parties were reported to have a strong odor of alcohol emanating, slur their words, be unsteady on their feet, and have argued with other parties on scene. The officers asked the male to sit due to swaying, found the two parties were from out of state without anyone else present

to take care of them, and thus placed them into protective custody. Bassam Geha, manager, was present for this discussion. Chairperson Angus asked if the manager’s bartenders had noticed that the two parties were previously intoxicated before being served. The manager explained he was not initially present at the scene of the incident as he was attending to the restaurant part of the business, whereas the incident occurred in the function hall, but immediately noticed upon arrival that the two parties were incapable of moving and asked how many drinks they had been served. Mr. Geha claimed that the cause of their state was likely due to drugs taken prior, and that an officer on scene seemed to agree. He said that the male party initially appeared to be compliant when offered an Uber ride home, but then suddenly became argumentative and started swearing and using slurs. This was when he asked his manager, Michael Murphy, to call the police. Commissioner Koutoulas asked how long the two parties had been present at the bar. Michael Murphy, who was in attendance, said he believed they were present from 9PM to 10:45PM. The Commissioner then echoed Chairperson Angus’ prior question as to whether the bartenders noticed any unsober behavior before serving or not. Mr. Geha said he couldn’t answer the question as he was not present at the time of serving, but the bartender on duty, the manager’s brother-in-law, told him they “were fine” initially before their behavior deteriorated. Commissioner Koutoulas noted that the police report did mention a strong odor of alcohol, even if drugs may have been involved. The manager said the bartenders did not notice anything when they served the two parties and that camera footage could be reviewed. He added that each party individually came once to acquire alcohol at the bar. Commissioner Koutoulas asked who served them and Mr. Geha answered it was his brother-in-law and that he was TIPS certified.

Chairperson Angus asked Sgt. Lynch if it were possible that the alcohol odor was coming from spilled drinks. The Sergeant replied that “anything was possible,” and that typically people consuming a substance would be affected overtime unless it was directly ingested. He added that the mix with a substance and alcohol could cause an additive or antagonistic effect, and that bar staff should remain vigilant to any potential signs of impairment before serving. The Chairperson then asked the manager if anyone else was on duty aside from the bartender: the manager clarified that he himself was in the restaurant section of the business while acting manager Michael Murphy was present in the Function Hall. The Chairperson further asked how many people were in attendance, to which the manager replied “less than 100”. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if Mr. Murphy and the brother-in-law were the only employees present at the time of the incident and Mr. Geha said they were, plus one

bartender, but that there were two people supervising that night’s show. The Chairperson asked if these latter two were his employees, and the manager explained they were outside staff working for an entertainment company. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if Mr. Geha had any security or bouncers working, and he replied the two entertainment people were usually at the door checking tickets. The Commissioner then expressed her concern that three employees didn’t seem sufficient to monitor 100 people. The manager said most people usually sit at tables and not at the bar and felt he didn’t need to hire a bouncer as “normally [they] don’t have any issues” but always called the Police Department if there were – Commissioner Koutoulas responded that it was preferable to curtail such issues before needing to involve the police. Chairperson Angus agreed that while it was a good decision to call the police, such events like the concert in question should have more staff present to monitor attendees as bartenders are too busy serving drinks. Sergeant Kevin Lynch inquired if patrons are allowed to exit the building and reenter. The manager said they “try not to” let that occur, and the Sergeant explained this should be taken into consideration in case patrons are taking substances outdoors before reentering. Commissioner Koutoulas added that she would like to see “No Reentry” adopted in the business’ policy. Mr. Geha said usually only smokers are those who might exit and reenter – the Commissioner suggested adding a roped off area for smokers and Chairperson Angus suggested adding a security detail outside to monitor those that briefly exit to smoke.

Motion: Commissioner Driscoll motions to place the matter on file. Second: Commissioner Koutoulas seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.

14.1 Michael DiFeo III, Manager

Mr. Mike's Restaurant & Lounge, Inc.

d/b/a Mr. Mike's Restaurant & Lounge

1149 Main Street

-Show cause hearing: Alleged violation of 204 CMR 2.05 (2) Permitting an illegality on the licensed premises to wit

-Incident that occurred on or about March 4, 2025, at 16:51

Sergeant Kevin Lynch presented the facts. On March 4th, 2025, at approximately 4:51PM, Officer Powell was dispatched to Mr. Mike’s Restaurant & Lounge after being called by the manager on duty, Kelsie Peabody, for a reported highly intoxicated male attempting to drive away. The officer stopped the car and noticed a strong odor of alcohol emanating from the driver. His eyes were “bloodshot and

glassy” and he was slurring his speech. The male told the officer he was coming from AMVETS where he had two vodka sodas. The officer advised him that he was coming from Mr. Mike’s and brought him to the hospital due to his level of intoxication. A criminal complaint was filed in court. Kelsie Peabody informed the officer that the male was a regular client on Tuesday evenings and that he was served a single beer that was “quickly taken away from him” due to his unusual behavior. He refused her advice to get an Uber ride home and drove away before he was stopped by the police.

Michael DiFeo III, manager, was virtually present and sworn in. Chairperson Angus acknowledged that the bartender noticed the male’s level of intoxication and took the beer away but expressed her concern that he was served in the first place. The manager said that the male has had a limp, which was why nothing unusual was initially noticed by staff. He added that, after ordering a meal and the beer, staff noticed he “wasn’t himself” when he went to pay, took away the beer, and attempted to persuade him to not drive himself home. The manager stated camera footage was delivered to the police department and that they could not legally force the male to stay put. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if the establishment had ever had a problem in the past with this patron, to which the manager said they did not. The Commissioner then asked if the patron received the beer after he ate his meal: the manager (who wasn’t present the night of the incident) said he believed both were ordered together but that he was “under the impression” he only had one sip of the beer. Sergeant Lynch, based on camera footage, offered that the male entered the restaurant at 4:25PM, was served a draft at the bar, ordered food afterwards at 4:27PM which partially came out at 4:31PM, the beer was removed at 4:47PM, and the male exited the restaurant at 4:49PM. Commissioner Koutoulas noted the importance of having cameras.

Kelsie Peabody, the manager working the night of the incident, was physically present and sworn in. She answered Chairperson Angus that she was not the one to serve the male but was the one to try and persuade him to not drive off. She added that the server spoke to her to explain the situation, and that it was her understanding that the male had had “two sips total” of the beer. Commissioner Koutoulas stated she believed the incident was handled properly and Commissioner Driscoll agreed. Motion: Commissioner Driscoll motions to place the matter on file. Second: Commissioner Koutoulas seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.

•Alleged Violation of MGL 138 § 34D Failure to display required OUI Alcohol Poster

•Alleged Violation of 204 CMR 2.01(10) Failure to display Alcohol License •Alleged Violation of City of Haverhill Ordinance § 100-5 Failure to comply with TIPS Training Requirement

-Incident that occurred on or about March 20, 2025, at 17:12

[Chairperson Angus prefaced the item by disclosing that HPD Detective Douglas Angus, involved in this incident, is her son and asked if anyone had objections to her sitting in on the hearing. No objections]

Sergeant Kevin Lynch presented the facts. The Haverhill Police Department conducted an underage alcohol compliance-check operation with the help of five operatives under 21 years old. At approximatively 5:12PM, two underage operatives each ordered and purchased a beer. When asked for IDs, an operative said “I’ll go get it” before the employee said “never mind”. This employee that completed the sale was only 17 years old, however the beers were served by a different employee – over 18 years of age but not TIPS certified. At this point, Sgt. Lynch was notified and arrived at La Pizza DiForno – the manager on duty, Sokol Qirjako, had since returned after being temporarily absent during the sale. Sgt. Lynch noted three missing items legally required to be displayed: the Liquor License, an OUI Violations poster, and an Underage Drinking Violations poster. He repeated that neither employee involved in the handling of the beers were TIPS certified.

Evangellos Asprogiannis, manager, was sworn in. He said the 17-year-old employee knew he was required to ask for a TIPS certified manager when patrons asked for alcohol and that two such managers were present that evening, despite the employee’s failure to do so. He also explained that the other employee that handled the beers was recently hired and being trained to use the registers, and therefore not yet TIPS certified. He agreed there was “no excuse” for the minor’s actions. The manager then assumed responsibility for the failure to display his liquor license. He explained that its frame had broken and he neglected to repair it, but did so the day following the incident. As for the posters, he said he could not locate them and reached out to the License Commission Clerk for assistance – he has since received two new copies that are now up for display. He provided proof of all three to Sgt. Lynch. Additionally, the manager stated that, on the same night of the incident, he had the manager on duty lock the alcohol cooler which can now only be unlocked by a manager or TIPS certified employee. He also said he had 6 employees do an in-person TIPS certification course in November. Commissioner Koutoulas verified if Mr. Asprogiannis was the manager on record and if Sokol Qirjako was TIPS certified – the manager confirmed both. The Commissioner asked if Mr. Qirjako was present at the time of the incident – the manager said he thought he was but Kevin Lynch stated he had been absent to run an errand. The manager added that a different TIPS certified employee was present but did not notice the sale as he was attending to another task, though admitting there was “no excuse”. Mr. Asprogiannis said he chose to not terminate the 17-year-old employee but assured the Commission that he would not reoffend. Commissioner Driscoll noted the manager’s efforts following the incident and remarked this was the business’ first offense in 7 years. Commissioner Koutoulas expressed she believed the business should be placed on a short probation. She then asked if the employee who handled the beers during the incident will be TIPS recertified in-person – the manager responded that their employment had actually been terminated, but that he recently hired a new employee that was TIPS certified. He also said he planned to redo an in-person course in the Fall, perhaps a joint venture with other Haverhill organizations – Commissioner Koutoulas and Chairperson Angus agreed it was a good idea. The Chairperson noted the manager’s proactivity following the incident and echoed that this was his first offence in 7 years.

Motion: Commissioner Driscoll motions to place La Pizza DiForno on a 3-month probation, until July 3rd, 2025.

Second: Commissioner Koutoulas seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.

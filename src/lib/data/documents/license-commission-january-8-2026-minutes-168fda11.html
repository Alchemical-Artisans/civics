6.1 Jessica LeBoeuf, Proposed Manager

Mac Daddies & a Little More LLC

d/b/a Mac Daddies & a Little More

75 South Main Street

-Application for new Entertainment license for Monday-Thursday & Saturday 8AM-1AM, Friday 8AM-2AM, and Sunday 10AM-1AM for Dancing (by patrons only), D.J., Instrumental Music, Jukebox, Karaoke, Music – Amplified, Sporting Event, Television, Comedy Show – 1 Comedian, Performance (includes live bands) – 3 Performers/Instruments, 1 Vocalist

-Has Police Department and Entertainment Zoning approval

Items 6.1, 7.1, and 9.1 were all heard together. Motions were made separately. Jessica LeBoeuf, proposed manager, and Atty. Tim Connors, representing Mac Daddies & a Little More LLC, were present for these applications. Chairperson Angus asked Ms. LeBoeuf about their experience in the business. Ms. LeBoeuf said she has been in the restaurant business for over 20 years, having spent most of that time at the former Uno Pizzeria & Grill in Haverhill as a server, bartender, and shift supervisor,

and now has her own business with Mac Daddies & a Little More. Atty. Connors and Ms. LeBoeuf explained that the business originated on 741 South Main St as a quick- service type of restaurant, before they had the opportunity to move over to 75 South Main St at the former Big Dawg’s Eatery location. Atty. Connors stated that they have been in business for about two years and transitioned to 75 South Main St “since November without issue and their business is doing very well.” He added that they were operating under the current owner’s Liquor License and that Ms. LeBoeuf’s fiancé, David Jackson, was absent from the meeting as he was currently at the business but had over 35 years of restaurant experience. The attorney concluded by stating the business was looking to get full licenses in its name, has been serving alcohol since November without issue, that they are a “restaurant first, bar second,” and that Ms. LeBoeuf and staff are TIPS certified.

Commissioner Koutoulas inquired if the business was currently serving alcohol and if staff were currently employed by the current owner, Robert Lapierre. Ms. LeBoeuf asked if the Commissioner was referring to the previous staff from Big Dawg’s Eatery. Chairperson Angus verified that they were operating under Mr. Lapierre’s licenses, and the applicant party confirmed so. The Chairperson then asked if Mr. Lapierre was still present on the premises and Ms. LeBoeuf replied they were working under him. The Chairperson clarified that this was not what she asked and asked if he was physically on location. Ms. LeBoeuf replied that he was, though not every day. The Chairperson expressed her concern that the Commission could not issue a liquor license to a location that is already licensed but said that the City Solicitor had advised that the Commission could approve the application “subject to the immediate surrender of the license from Mr. Lapierre upon approval by the ABCC.” Atty. Connors replied that he had not had the opportunity to inquire about Mr. Lapierre but that it was his understanding that this was the applying party’s intention. Chairperson Angus remarked that generally an applicant would go through a Transfer application with this type of situation. The attorney agreed that it was unusual and would have “done things differently” if he had been solicited sooner, but that he appreciates the Commission working with his client. The Chairperson expressed her concern that the Commission should not even be approving a new license application but could under the aforementioned stipulation. Commissioner Koutoulas noted that there were a number of documents outstanding and inquired about their status. Attorney Connors said he had only heard a few and asked if the Commission had the Abutters’ Notification Affidavit. Clerk Flynn clarified that the affidavit was submitted and that the “final requirements” referred to the requirement of the applicant party to submit their signed green cards from their certified mail, once they had received them back. Chairperson Angus added that she would like

a copy of the business’ NOV bank statement as the statements they received were from August-October and Ms. LeBoeuf agreed. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if they had scheduled their 304 Occupancy. Chairperson Angus clarified that this was the business occupancy for their liquor license, and they would need to apply for their own once their application was approved and they no longer operated under Mr. Lapierre’s license. Commissioner Koutoulas added that all outstanding documents would need to be provided before the license could be issued if/when approved, such as the Liquor Liability and Workers’ Compensation insurances. Ms. LeBoeuf asked Clerk Flynn if he didn’t already have these – Clerk Flynn replied that the Workers’ Compensation insurance was submitted after he had posted the meeting agenda, but that the affidavit was “still coming.” Ms. LeBoeuf said they did have Liquor Liability and asked if Clerk Flynn had a copy. Clerk Flynn replied he did not have a copy of insurance under Mac Daddies & a Little More’s name, but had an insurance policy under Big Dawg’s Eatery, LLC’s name.

Regarding the Entertainment license, Chairperson Angus advised Ms. LeBoeuf that any performers would count against their occupancy number. Regarding the bank statements, Atty. Connors stated he could submit their NOV & DEC bank statements. Motion: Commissioner Koutoulas motions to approve the new Entertainment license for Monday-Thursday & Saturday 8AM-1AM, Friday 8AM-2AM, and Sunday 10AM- 1AM for Dancing (by patrons only), D.J., Instrumental Music, Jukebox, Karaoke, Music – Amplified, Sporting Event, Television, Comedy Show – 1 Comedian, Performance (includes live bands) – 3 Performers/Instruments, 1 Vocalist. Second: Commissioner Driscoll seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.

15.1 Discussion on potential revision of new TIPS regulations established on June 5, 2025

Chairperson Angus mentioned she had previously read an email from Commissioner Koutoulas regarding this matter and found the proposed amendment to be reasonable. She added that they did not want to hurt downtown businesses but ensure they receive proper training, and that it was a “useful tool” for businesses summoned before the Commission for “other matters.”

Commissioner Koutoulas summarized the proposal: new license-holders and new Managers on Record would be required to follow the new regulations (i.e. in-person or approved, monitored online training), but any business that currently had no issues and was not on probation would be allowed to use the online, nonmonitored TIPS course. However, if there were to be a “subsequent violation” for any business, they would then be forced to recertify following the requirements of the new regulations. If such a business were in good standing in the future, they would be allowed to return to the unmonitored online training. Commissioner Driscoll commented that this was “comprehensive and fair”.

Motion: Commissioner Koutoulas motions to amend the new TIPS regulations as stated above.

Second: Commissioner Driscoll seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.

-Item for Discussion: Status of Video System Operationality

Gina N., Ms. Holt’s sister-in-law, appeared on behalf of The Nerve. Gina shared that Ms. Holt and Mr. Bentham apologized for their absence and were currently at a contracted event they could not get out of. Gina said the business was working “diligently” on finding different service providers and were trying to “work within the limits of what they’re given”. The lieutenant replied it was usually an internal system with a hard drive that the Police Department could download footage

from. He also remarked that the previous owners did not have a similar problem as The Nerve. • Three (3) cameras on premises at the following locations – entrance, exit, and

-Item for Discussion: Incident that occurred on or about May 8, 2026 at 8:12PM Lt. Kevin Lynch presented the facts: on May 8th, an officer noticed a male party on the ground in front of the establishment. The party allegedly suddenly began acting erratically and throwing himself on the ground before exiting the establishment, approximately 15 minutes before the officer arrived. The party assaulted the officer and was transported to the hospital for medical treatment and later summoned to court. The lieutenant said he did review footage of the incident, and was concerned that the establishment never called the police and allowed the patron to leave the bar and be left on the ground outside.

Jonus Rustani was present and asked if no one had really called the police. Lt. Lynch advised that the officer happened to notice the party as he was driving by. Commissioner Koutoulas asked Mr. Rustani if he worked that evening. The manager stated he did and had noticed the patron’s erratic behavior, which he described as a “medical episode,” and did not remember if he called the police or not but was trying to restrain the party along with the help of patrons. The Commissioner asked if the manager “would have” called the police and the manager said he would have but was concerned about what the patron might do to anyone inside his bar. Commissioner Driscoll reiterated that a police officer was hurt as a result. Commissioner Koutoulas asked if there were two employees working that night since it was a Friday – Mr. Rustani replied he wasn’t sure if his brother was there at that time, despite his brother “know[ing] he’s supposed to be there,” and could “not control him”. The Commissioner asked if his brother was a part-owner of the business – Mr. Rustani replied he was not as of January and removed as a trustee. The Commissioner continued saying the manager was the sole owner of the bar and knew that two bartenders are required Thursday-Sunday nights, and that his brother not showing up was not his brother’s problem but the owner’s responsibility. Mr. Rustani said he understood that but all he could do was tell him when he’s supposed to be working. Commissioner Koutoulas countered that he needed a backup plan should he fail to come, and that having two bartenders helps ensure safety. The manager asked what he was supposed to do if he told his brother to come and he did not – the Commissioner replied he should then fire him and hire a new person. Chairperson Laliberty said she had reviewed the business’ history and pointed out that these type of issues are

common for such an establishment – Mr. Rustani interjected that his brother had his own issues. The Chairperson replied that, regardless, the manager is required to have two people working four nights a week and he is ultimately responsible. Commissioner Koutoulas noted that the male party from the incident did not appear to be drinking and Mr. Rustani said he does not “drink at all”. He continued saying that the party appeared to be fine at first but then starting speaking incoherently and rolling around on the floor. A different patron then picked him up and forced him outside; the party attempted to come back inside but the manager and other patrons kept him out. Mr. Rustani said he was concerned that the party would hurt himself or others inside. Commissioner Koutoulas noted that the patron that forced the party outside made themselves potentially liable for injuries, and that it was not any patron’s responsibility to deal with such incidents. Chairperson Laliberty added that this is what the police is for, and the manager is required to call them when such an incident occurs. Mr. Rustani rhetorically asked if he should “pick the phone up” or “try to prevent [harm] first”. Commissioners Koutoulas and Driscoll emphasized this is why the business was mandated to have two staff members four nights a week: one can call while the other intervenes. Mr. Rustani replied: “Whatever you…recommend.” Commissioner Koutoulas stated she believed the license should be revoked. Mr. Rustani said the situation was very hard on him too. The Commissioner acknowledged that it was very difficult to manage a business alone and could only imagine all the difficulties involved for a bar owner. She added that the situation was “heartbreaking” but also disturbing as a License Commissioner, and that it seemed to be

too overwhelming for Mr. Rustani. Mr. Rustani expressed he wished to sell the property.

The following motion encompassed both Items 13.3 & 13.4:

Motion: Commissioner Koutoulas motions to permanently revoke The New Lantern Cafe, Inc.’s §12 Common Victualler’s Liquor License and Entertainment License, effective immediately, and to provide the Clerk a receipt of sale of all remaining alcohol on the premises back to the vendor, by July 10, 2026.

Second: Commissioner Driscoll seconded.

Vote: 3-0-0 vote unanimous, motion passed.
